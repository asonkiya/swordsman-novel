# Chapter 6

Tianyi's hand came down on his arm and stopped him mid-stride. "Wait."

Jianxin stopped. Through the trees an escort took a hit across the shoulder and adjusted without going down. He was good. Both of them were good. It wasn't going to matter.

"Thirty-two visible, more in the trees maybe," Tianyi said, already reading. "The escorts are seventh level Qi Refining, trained, the real thing. The bandits run from fourth to seventh and you can see the difference, worse steel, looser feet, relying on numbers. Someone on a horse at the far edge, hasn't drawn. The ones hanging back keep looking at him." He tracked the back of the group. "The carriage is what they came for. The escorts know it. They're not pushing through, just holding the line around it."

That was the whole analysis. He said nothing further.

The carriage door opened, and a woman stepped down, which took a certain composure given what was happening fifteen meters off. Tall, dark-haired, and she had already seen them, her eyes moving fast over the two of them, taking inventory. She said something to the nearer escort without looking away from the treeline, and the escort raised his voice.

"You, in the trees. House Vayne of Aldenmere pays well for capable hands."

Jianxin looked at Tianyi. Tianyi looked at Jianxin. Tianyi moved toward the carriage. Jianxin moved toward the bandits, and the hand left his arm, and he was already grinning, weight forward on the balls of his feet.

---

Tianyi came in from the side, unhurried, which confused the three men who peeled off to meet him. By the time they understood that unhurried did not mean slow, the earth under the carriage had shifted, anchoring its wheels, and a wall of wind that wasn't quite visible had settled around it. He did not so much fight the three men as rearrange the ground they stood on. The first found his lead foot sunk to the ankle in dirt that had been packed hard a breath ago, and while he worked that out the air in front of his face thickened and stopped being easy to breathe. The second came in fast, and the wind bent the line of his charge a few degrees so he arrived where Tianyi was not, and the earth came up to meet him there. The third watched the first two and stopped on his own. None of it looked like fighting. It looked like a man setting right a space that had been arranged wrong, and the men in it were part of what was wrong.

---

The moment Jianxin stepped onto the road the world before his eyes changed.

Not the road. Not the men. Those were the same. But layered over them, running through them like rivers through stone, was something he'd never seen in four days of forest and had been waiting for without knowing he waited. Every person on the road was carrying intent, the direction of a will moving through a body toward a conclusion, and he could see all of them at once, every current and eddy of the whole moving sea, and not one of them lost in the rest.

The big one at the front was aimed past the escorts like they were furniture, his whole being pointed at the carriage. The one on the left flank was half gone already, his body still moving while his heart measured the distance to the trees. The young one on the right was committed to a lunge that hadn't left his body, intent already arrived while his feet caught up.

Jianxin's sword came out and he moved, around resistance rather than through it, finding the stillpoints between one current and the next, the brief silences where one will had finished and the next had not begun, where his blade could arrive before a man knew he'd been there.

The first one taught him that arriving was not finishing.

The young one's lunge passed the point of recall and Jianxin was already inside it, in the stillpoint the boy's own commitment had opened. The blade had only to fall. And for the length of a breath it didn't, because the reading hadn't stopped at the lunge. It had gone past it, into the boy himself, a whole person, shifting and unfinished, a life still deciding what it would be, all of it about to stop because Jianxin's arm was already moving. He had not once thought, until this instant, about what the blade was for.

His arm balked. Half a beat, no more.

It was enough. A cut he'd already discounted caught him across the ribs. The pain arrived and brought the road back with it, and the thing that had to happen happened. The blade went in beneath the arm, drew clear, and the boy folded around the space where it had been. One.

He was elsewhere before the body landed. An old general's footwork carried him through a gap and the man who'd made it took the edge across the neck before his swing finished. Two. A cut came from the right; the scholar-swordsman's guard turned it aside without pause and carried his point back through the man who'd thrown it. Three. The patient woman's arc, slow and certain, and the fourth man walked onto it himself. Four.

Then the rhythm changed. Six currents drew together into one, a tide where there'd been a river. The stillpoints closed.

He reached for it as he reached for everything, for the will that had pulled the six together, the place where the decision lived, so he could read it and step through it. And his gift closed on nothing. There was no will to find. The coherence was not in any of the six. It was in the space between them, in how each man's movement answered the others without any of them deciding it, and that space was the one thing on the whole road he could not read. He had been reaching into men his entire life. There was no man here to reach into.

The tide came in while he was still groping for a door that wasn't there, and a blade he never saw, thrown by a coordination and not a person, opened his side above the hip, deep enough that the heat of it arrived before the pain. He had not read it because there had been nothing to read. He gave ground, hand pressed to it, and the blood came through his fingers.

Tianyi's wind arrived in two lines, not a strike but a question, and two of the six had to answer it, and the tide lost its coherence for a single beat. Jianxin went with it. He took the line it gave him before it could close, split the next two before the gap shut, and three men with three separate wills were just men again, each one legible, and the road received what the road received.

The man on the horse made the same calculation Jianxin had read on the left-flank man's face before any of it began. They broke, faster than the tide had formed. The horse went. The last of them found the treeline, and the road went quiet.

Jianxin lowered his sword. His side was open and warm and his hand had not left it. He looked at the men on the road.

The reading was gone out of them. Not the bodies, the silence where the currents had been, all those wills that had each been moving somewhere and now moved nowhere and would not begin again. He'd felt it reach for him once, in the half-beat before the first of them, and set it aside because the alternative was dying with it. Now there was nothing left to set it aside for. He stood with it. It sat heavier than he'd expected, and it did not pass off him as the ache in his ribs would. The young one had been a whole person a breath before he wasn't, and Jianxin had felt the whole of him, and could not now unfeel it. Everyone he ever cut down he would have read first, and would carry out whole, this one and every one after it, into a memory that did not let go of anyone on this world. There would be no setting them down.

Tianyi appeared at his shoulder. "Fourteen down. The rest are gone." He looked at his brother's face and did not say the other thing.

The two escorts stood breathing hard, the wounded one's hand pressed to his shoulder, both looking at Jianxin. The carriage door opened and the woman came down again, and this time walked past the escorts toward the brothers. One moved to intercept her and she stopped him with a look. She came on and stopped a few meters off.

"Thank you," she said, and meant it, and held it. And underneath the thanks, running at the same time and not troubling to hide that it ran, was the arithmetic. Two unaffiliated fighters had walked out of the trees onto this road, at this hour, the one hour her carriage was being taken by thirty-two men someone had paid. Jianxin watched her hold the gratitude in one hand and the coincidence in the other and not let either one cost the other anything.

"Forgive me. You're very young."

"Sixteen." Jianxin found a grin for her, the easy one, though just now it cost a little more than usual.

She didn't return it. Up close she was older than the treeline had made her, eighteen or nineteen, though she'd been managing things long enough that it showed. "Where were you headed?"

"Northeast," Tianyi said. "Toward people."

"That would be Aldenmere." A pause. "And you came out of the forest. Jianxin. And Tianyi." She weighed the names. "Those aren't from here. Not Ordered Lands names, we don't sound like that under the Arbiter. Not Storied either, I've sat across from half the old Witness houses and none of them name a son that way." Her eyes went to the fourteen on the road. "The Open Lands was where the names put you. But their fighters come at a thing as though it makes no odds whether they walk away from it, and the two of you came onto this road like the outcome was the only thing in the world." She looked up. "Not Ordered, not Storied, not Open. That's most of the map. So where does that leave you."

"Somewhere the map doesn't trouble with," Jianxin said, light as anything. "We grew up a long way from anyone's god. Outside the whole of it, more or less."

The first crack went through her composure. Not suspicion. Nearer to alarm. "There is no outside the whole of it. Not on this side of the mountain." A beat. "Unless you mean the Den."

Neither of them answered. She took the silence for a yes, and Jianxin let her keep it; a lost mountain tribe was a tidier story than the truth, and a good deal easier to tell. "You came down off the mountain." Quieter, working something large back through itself. "There are tribes up there still? I'd have set money on the last of them being gone a generation past. Nothing lives that close to the Den and goes on living." Her eyes went over them and arrived somewhere she plainly did not enjoy. "Though it would account for you. Nothing soft comes down that slope."

She let it sit, and when she spoke again the courtesy was back with a floor under it. "I'm Maren Vayne. My family holds Aldenmere. A coordinated force just tried to take me off this road, and the two who broke it walked out of a forest with no house and a name from the wrong side of the mountain. You'll understand I can't thank you and wave you off. You'll come back to Aldenmere and account for yourselves to my family. After that, where you go is your own affair."

It was not phrased as a question.

The grin stayed and lost its ease. "We took thirty-two men off your carriage not an hour ago," he said. "I'd have thought that was accounting enough. I came out from under sixteen years of being told where to stand this morning, and I wasn't looking to start the afternoon on it."

Tianyi's hand found his arm. He leaned in, low, under the road and the woman both. "We know nothing about this place. Not what she is, not what stands behind her, not what it costs to make an enemy of either. Everything we have we got out of books, and the books did not cover this. We can leave whenever we like. We can't take it back once we've made them an enemy."

Jianxin let the breath out. The grin came back, rueful at the edges. "Aldenmere, then. Since we were headed that way regardless." He held her eye. "Walking in on our own feet. Not summoned."

Maren looked between them, having caught more of that than they'd meant her to, and let it pass. "Good."
