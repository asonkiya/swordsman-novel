# Chapter 10

A servant found them in the corridor. Lady Maren's study, when they were ready.

The study was three doors down from the council room. Maren stood at the window with her back to them and turned when they came in, and Jianxin caught it in the first second before the composure reasserted, the set of her shoulders, how she'd been holding herself while she waited. On the road, at swordpoint, she had been steadier than this. It had shifted now.

She sat. They sat.

"This is why I hired you," she said. "Not the escort work. I need support from the vassal tier. Enough to call a formal authority review without it collapsing before it starts. Drevant, Serrath, Orath. Each has something that needs to move. I need you to make that happen."

Tianyi had stopped looking at the bookshelves, his eyes moving somewhere past the spines, quick and unfocused, a man reading several things at once that only he could see.

"Are you going to tell us what the authority review is for?" Jianxin said.

She looked at him. "Not particularly."

"Emergency authority under Clause Fourteen," Tianyi said.

The room went very quiet.

"Your father hasn't used his co-signature in four years. Not on anything that counts. There's a provision. Dormancy. If the authority's lapsed, it passes to you by default. You don't need the vote. You may already have it." He turned a page on the shelf without removing it from it. "The vassal work isn't building toward a vote. It's clearing the ground so that when you move, there's nothing in the room that can stop it cheaply." He looked at her for the first time. "It's a far-fetched plan."

Maren was looking at him, openly shocked. Her uncle had been blocking her at every turn for months and hadn't found it.

"You read the charter."

"The copy in the library. Two days ago."

"The full document."

"It took an afternoon."

Something reset in her face, small, controlled. She had been the person who knew the documents in every room for two years, and she adjusted to no longer being it without comment.

"I've ruled out everything that can't be done," she said. "The rest is just doing it."

Jianxin had heard that before, or the shape of it. Not the words but the structure under them, the frictionless certainty of someone who had genuinely eliminated the alternatives rather than suppressed them. Garson had written like that. The reasoning moves because it has nowhere else to go.

"What are you trying to protect?" he said.

"The family's position. The marriage—"

"House Edrath," Tianyi said.

She looked at him.

"The Edrath name comes up three times in the last year's correspondence index. Old name. Witness-aligned. The match gives the family cover it can't produce on its own." He said it without inflection.

Maren was quiet. "Yes." A pause. "My sister is twelve. She hasn't decided yet what kind of person to be." She stopped there.

The room held it. Jianxin thought about the girl by the window, the one who'd looked down at her hands when he asked for somewhere to sleep, and set the thought beside the marriage, and did not like the shape they made together.

"And Drevant," he said. "He withdrew this morning. That's the support you needed first, and it's gone."

"It's gone." Her jaw set. "And I've had since the council to decide whether Casver pushed him or whether he didn't need pushing, and I can't tell which."

"He didn't need pushing," Jianxin said. "And it isn't Casver, and it isn't you. It's the killings on his land."

She went still.

"Your staff told us in the corridor. Seven dead since spring, in the houses of the devout, in their beds, and his watch can't touch it." He leaned forward. "I read him today, Maren. He didn't pull away from you out of grievance, and your uncle didn't get to him. Something's killing his people and he can't stop it, and a man like that can't sit in a court arguing roads while it happens. He's not your enemy. He's the one vassal in this house you don't have to worry about."

Tianyi said nothing at first. Then: "The killings are on his land. His watchmen aren't."

Maren looked at him. "No. They're on our border. He gave them to me when I asked, in the spring, before any of this. I have his strength sitting on my line, and his villages are being emptied a bed at a time without it."

"Then the road back to him runs through the killings," Tianyi said, "not through the vassal court. Stop what's killing his people and you've shown his land is safer with this house than without it. He won't need persuading after that. A man like the one my brother just described goes where his oath sends him."

"After the engagement," Maren said. "Not before. The Edrath dinner is in six days and Casver will use every hour of those six. The killings are a campaign. They'll keep." She drew a breath. "One thing at a time."

She looked between them. "The dinner first. Be in the room. Whatever ground shifts that night, my uncle moves on it by morning, and I need eyes that can see the people even if they can't see the rest."

Jianxin stood. He looked at her, at the hands flat on the desk, at the dark circles under the precise hair, at the face that had been sealed when she stepped out of a carriage on a forest road and was sealed again now.

Almost.

"We'll be in the room," he said.

She nodded once, and the session was closed.

---

They walked back through the corridor. The household staff moved around them quietly, knowing when not to ask questions.

"You gave her Drevant," Tianyi said. "Flat. No hedge."

"Because I'm sure of him." Jianxin glanced over. "You're not."

"I'm sure of the man you read." Tianyi looked ahead. "I'll want the records on his land before the dinner. The route his trade runs on."

"It's the killings. I felt it. There's no bottom to the grief in him over those villages."

"I believe you." Tianyi walked on a few steps. "I'd still like the figures."

"Read the man right and you know what he'll do."

"Usually," Tianyi said, and let it lie.

Jianxin almost pushed it, and didn't, because his brother wasn't arguing, only setting a thing down between them and leaving it there.
