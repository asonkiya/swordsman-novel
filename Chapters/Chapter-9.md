# Chapter 9

The forest had no opinion about them whatsoever.

Jianxin had expected something. Not a welcome exactly, but some quality of difference, the world asserting itself, the air tasting like freedom, something that marked the transition from one kind of life to another. What he got was trees. Very large trees, close together, and none of them cared.

He stood there for a moment taking it in. The light came through the canopy in long diagonals, pale and directionless, the same starless quality as the sky above. The air smelled like soil and something faintly green. Somewhere nearby water was moving over stones.

It was genuinely beautiful. He gave it that.

Then Tianyi was already twenty meters ahead, and Jianxin followed him.

Tianyi moved through the forest with the unhurried certainty of someone who had read extensively about forests and found the reality consistent with expectations. He paused at intervals to check something Jianxin couldn't see, adjust his bearing, continue. Within the first hour he had located the stream by sound, identified two viable shelter sites by the angle of the trees and the dryness of the ground, and determined that the undergrowth composition suggested they were within a day or two of habitation. Somewhere in the middle of all this, the tension Tianyi had been carrying in his shoulders since leaving the institute quietly left him.

Jianxin walked behind him and watched the forest and waited for something interesting to happen.

By midday nothing interesting had happened. He'd tried twice to reach for the living things around him the way he'd learned to reach, looking for the thing underneath the surface expression, and found exactly nothing. A deer crossed fifty meters ahead and he'd reached for it automatically and found hunger and alertness and nothing he could work with. The forest was full of life and completely useless to him.

He went hunting instead, which at least gave him something to do.

---

They made camp at the better of the two shelter sites Tianyi had identified. By the time Jianxin returned with two rabbits, badly skinned, a lean-to stood at the edge of a small clearing, correctly angled, positioned for wind, close enough to the stream for water and far enough to stay dry. Tianyi had apparently retained all three of the field construction texts he'd read during the institute years.

"You didn't have to do all that," Jianxin said.

"No," Tianyi agreed.

Jianxin handed him a rabbit. Tianyi cooked it with a precise application of fire qi that left the meat cooked through without charring the outside, which was annoying.

They ate. The forest settled into its evening quiet and the sky went from orange to purple to the deep familiar blue. Jianxin poked at the fire and thought about people he hadn't met yet and what they might be carrying inside them.

"Have you tried reading the animals?" Tianyi said.

"Tried a deer. Nothing useful."

"A deer should be simpler than a person. Less complexity, fewer variables. If anything your approach should work better."

"That's not how it works."

"Why not."

Jianxin thought about how to explain it. "It's not about complexity. A deer doesn't have intent. It has hunger and fear and instinct, but none of that is intent in the sense I can work with. Intent requires someone deciding something. A deer doesn't decide anything. It just is."

Tianyi considered this. "So the limiting variable isn't cognitive complexity but the presence of deliberate will."

"Something like that."

"That's a significant constraint on your path."

"Only out here." Jianxin looked at the trees. "How far do you think we are from people?"

Tianyi opened the cultivation manual the clan had sent him, read for a moment, then closed it and looked at the treeline. "There's a stream gradient that suggests drainage toward lower terrain to the northeast. The undergrowth density has been decreasing since midday, which usually indicates prior human clearing. The terrain itself is interesting." He paused. "The ground has a consistent incline in all directions."

"We're on a hill."

"Possibly. But the angle is consistent across too wide an area for a typical hill. If the gradient continued at this rate the structure would be unfathomably large. I'm assuming it flattens out further from here." He turned back to the manual. "Northeast. Two days at the outside."

Jianxin looked at the ground around them, which looked like ordinary ground. He'd grown up on a world with a slope and had stopped noticing it at some point. Hearing it described from outside was strange. "Fine. Northeast."

"Probably less than two days," Tianyi said, and began reading.

---

The night passed. Jianxin woke to Tianyi already up and sitting cross-legged outside the lean-to, hands on his knees, eyes closed. Not asleep.

He'd said the night before that he was going to try.

Jianxin sat up and watched.

The change, when it came, was not visible. Tianyi's posture didn't shift. His breathing stayed even. But something about the air around him had altered, a faint denseness, as if the space around Tianyi had become marginally more present than the space around everything else. Jianxin noticed it without being able to name it and kept watching.

What Tianyi was doing: he had chosen something he understood thoroughly, the way cold air behaves around flame, and was attempting to make it real within the dantian rather than merely understood. Not to produce it externally. Just to cross the threshold between the virtual and the real, to hold it as a living thing rather than the idea of one.

The manual had described this. What the manual had not described was what it felt like when something crossed that threshold.

He had expected to draw something inward. That was the language the texts used, gathering, accumulating, filling. What he felt was nothing like that. The real and the virtual were not opposites pulling against each other but yin and yang, each containing the seed of the other. The flame he had made real within the dantian needed qi the way any real thing needs the world to exist in, not attracted but required, the qi simply present because the real thing was present and reality demands its due.

The sensation was weight without mass. The difference between a map and the territory it describes, felt from the inside. He had known how cold air and flame moved together since his first year of study. For eleven minutes he was the place where that knowledge stopped being knowledge and became fact.

Then it slipped back into the virtual, and the sensation faded.

When he opened his eyes Jianxin was still watching.

"Well?" Jianxin said.

Tianyi considered how to describe it. "You know the difference between reading about a place and standing in it."

"Yes."

"It's like that. But the place is inside you."

Jianxin was quiet for a moment. "That's almost a good description."

"It's the best I have." Tianyi looked at his hands. "It's more demanding than I anticipated. The real wants to slip back into the virtual constantly. Holding something on this side of that threshold requires unbroken attention. The texts underestimated this."

"So you can't just do it continuously."

"Not yet." He stood and picked up his bag. "I'll try something deeper tonight."

---

The second day was the same as the first except Jianxin got better at skinning rabbits and Tianyi ran six distinct workings through the dantian, each more complex than the last, and reported at the end of it that the maintenance cost was decreasing as expected.

The third day they sparred for two hours in a clearing because there was nothing else to do with his cultivation and combat was at least something Jianxin could be interested in. Tianyi's elemental control in open forest was more fluid than it had been in the institute's controlled spaces, wind moving differently between trees, fire with more fuel, earth underfoot denser and more responsive. Jianxin adapted to this faster than Tianyi had expected, which Tianyi noted and filed away.

They ate. Tianyi cultivated. Jianxin lay on his back and looked at the canopy and thought about people.

On the morning of the fourth day Tianyi stopped walking.

Jianxin almost walked into him. "What."

Tianyi held up a hand. His head had tilted a fraction of a degree, the angle of someone whose hearing had found something at the edge of its range.

"Fighting," he said.

Jianxin listened. The forest gave him nothing.

"How far?"

"Far enough that it has been going on for a while. Multiple combatants, not settled." He had already adjusted his bearing. "This direction."

They moved. The forest thinned, the canopy broke apart overhead, and between the trees ahead a road appeared in pieces, packed earth, wheel ruts, the signs of regular use. A carriage sat at an angle that suggested it had stopped suddenly rather than been parked. Two escorts still on their feet, holding, but the numbers around them were wrong by a wide margin. Dozens of men, more arriving from the treeline on the far side. The escorts were buying time and running out of it.

Jianxin's hand went to his sword before he'd decided to move it.

"We don't know anything about this situation," Tianyi said, beside him.

"No," Jianxin agreed, and kept walking toward it.
