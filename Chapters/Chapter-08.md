# Chapter 8

The senior instructor was waiting at the forest's edge.

He had the stillness of someone who has been waiting comfortably and would continue to wait as long as necessary. In his hand was a sealed package, smaller than the one the clan had sent Tianyi.

He held it out to Jianxin.

"A moment," he said. "Alone."

Tianyi walked into the trees without comment. Jianxin took the package and waited.

"You understand that you're not required to go with him," the instructor said. His voice was the same as it had always been, measured, unhurried, carrying the quality Jianxin had never been able to get beneath. "The clan has made arrangements. You could be with your family by the end of the month."

"I know," Jianxin said.

"Your brother's path will take years. Difficult years, most likely. The world outside this institute is not arranged for anyone's convenience."

"I know that too."

The instructor looked at him with an expression Jianxin still couldn't read. After seven years it remained as opaque as the first day, which was itself a kind of information, whatever was underneath it was not something that leaked.

"Then I have nothing else to say," the instructor said. He paused, and something shifted in his expression, small enough that Jianxin almost missed it. Almost. "Except that the Madam is pleased to see her sons support one another." Another pause, shorter than the first. "She misses you both severely. She would like very much to speak with you. When you're able."

He said it simply, without the measured quality the rest of his sentences carried, and it was the first time in seven years Jianxin had been able to get anything from him at all. He filed it away in a place he'd be able to find it later.

"Thank you," Jianxin said. "For all of it."

The instructor nodded once, which was more than Jianxin had expected.

He walked into the forest where Tianyi was waiting. His brother looked at the package, then at his face, and asked nothing.

---

The forest was enormous.

This was not a thought Jianxin had expected to have. He'd grown up knowing the world was large, had read about forests and plains and the vast territories of other cities, had understood in the abstract that the institute occupied a very small piece of a very large world. The understanding had been entirely abstract until now, when the trees were actually around him and the institute was actually gone and the world was actually starting.

"It's big," he said.

"Yes," Tianyi said.

"Bigger than I thought."

"The institute was approximately four hundred meters at its widest point. A standard forest extends for kilometers in multiple directions." He paused. "The scale difference is significant."

"Thank you, that's very helpful."

Tianyi looked at him. "You knew that."

"I did. It's different knowing it and standing in it."

Tianyi had already stopped paying attention to him. He was looking at the trees, at the undergrowth, at the angle of the light through the canopy. His head tilted slightly as he read the terrain, building whatever picture he was building. The tension he'd been carrying in his shoulders since the gate, since before the gate probably, loosened as he worked. The world was a system. Systems could be read. Jianxin watched him do it and felt the specific pleasure of watching his brother be immediately, completely himself in a new environment.

The morning light came through the canopy in long diagonals and the air smelled like things growing and things rotting and something else underneath both of those that Jianxin didn't have a word for. He breathed it in and found it good. He touched the bark of the nearest tree, then the one after it. Different textures. He hadn't known that.

"What are you doing," Tianyi said, without looking up from the undergrowth.

"Touching the trees."

"Why."

"I've never touched a tree before. Outside the institute grounds."

A pause. "They're the same trees."

"Different bark."

Tianyi looked up at that. He came over, put his hand on the first tree, then the second. Something in his expression shifted, the look he got when his model required updating.

"The composition of the soil is different on this side of the slope," he said. "That would affect the bark texture." He moved on. "Interesting."

Jianxin grinned at his back.

They walked. After a while the silence between them settled into the comfortable kind, both of them busy noticing things.

"The birds here are louder than I expected," Jianxin said.

"They're the same birds as in the institute grounds."

"They sound different out here."

Tianyi listened for a moment. "The acoustics are different. In the institute the sound was contained and reflected. Out here it disperses." He paused. "They are objectively the same volume."

"They sound louder."

"Subjectively."

"Yes, subjectively, that's what I said."

Tianyi didn't dignify this further.

Jianxin picked up a stick and hit a tree with it. A satisfying sound. He did it again.

"What are you doing now."

"Hitting a tree with a stick."

"Why."

"Because I can."

Tianyi looked at him for a moment. Then he picked up his own stick, tapped a tree once with mathematical precision, and set it down.

"All right," he said. "I see the appeal."

Jianxin laughed. It came out louder than he expected in the open air, no walls to contain it, and a nearby bird took off in alarm. He watched it go.

They walked a little further. A branch above them shed a few leaves that spiraled down through the light. Jianxin watched them fall and felt something he didn't have a name for yet. Not an ending. A first page.

"Tianyi."

"Yes."

"What do you think our parents are like."

A pause. Tianyi was choosing how to answer something he'd thought about before.

"Capable," he said finally. "Given what they produced and the choices they made. The decision to send us here required planning and sacrifice across years. That suggests methodical thinking and genuine commitment to a long-term outcome." He considered. "Beyond that I don't have sufficient information."

"I think our mother is funny," Jianxin said.

Tianyi looked at him. "On what basis."

"The message through the instructor. She misses us severely. The word severely. That's funny."

Tianyi thought about this. "Or precise."

"Both, maybe."

They walked in silence for a bit. Jianxin tried reaching for the birds and found nothing, the same absence he'd found with the deer in the institute grounds. He'd known this would be the case. He still tried.

"There's no one to read out here," he said.

"We'll find people."

"I know." He looked at the trees, which were very numerous and very unreadable. "I'm not complaining. Just noticing."

"You've been noticing things for the past hour."

"Everything is new. I'm allowed."

"You touched seventeen trees."

"You counted."

Tianyi said nothing.

"The terrain has a consistent incline," Tianyi said, after a moment. "Barely perceptible but present."

"Hills?"

"That would be the obvious explanation." He frowned slightly. "The gradient is wider than I'd expect for hills this size."

"Or it's just a really big mountain," Jianxin said.

"The scale would be unfathomably large. That's not possible on natural terrain." He filed it and kept walking. "Hills."

Jianxin looked at the ground, which looked like ordinary ground. He'd grown up on this world his whole life and had stopped noticing the slope entirely. It was strange to have it pointed out.

"We should find people soon," he said, looking up through the canopy.

"A day or two, based on the terrain. There will be a road."

"And then?"

Tianyi was quiet for a moment. "And then we find out what the world actually is instead of what we thought it was."

"That's the part I'm looking forward to," Jianxin said. He meant it completely. The world had been doing things while they weren't watching, accumulating people and problems and situations and intents he had never read, living its life without reference to the institute or the two of them. Now he was walking into it with a sword on his back and seven years of storybook sword arts and the first edges of something he could feel but not yet name, and he found the whole situation genuinely, thoroughly funny in the best possible way.

Tianyi looked at him sideways. "You're enjoying this."

"Severely."

"We have no money, no contacts, nowhere to sleep, and no plan."

"I know." Jianxin grinned. "It's great."

Tianyi was quiet for a moment. He looked at the trees ahead, at the light coming through the canopy, at the road that was somewhere beyond the forest waiting to be found. All of it unknown. All of it unmodeled. The variables he couldn't account for yet because he hadn't encountered them yet. He'd told Jianxin in the arena that it was the time between not knowing and figuring out that concerned him.

It was here now. He took a breath.

"Yes," he said. "All right."

Something at the corner of his expression moved in a direction that was not quite a smile and was not quite not one either. He kept walking.

Jianxin fell into step beside him, his bag light on his back, his sword familiar at his side, the forest enormous and alive around them and the world somewhere beyond it waiting to be found.

They walked into it together, shoulder to shoulder, as they had always been.
