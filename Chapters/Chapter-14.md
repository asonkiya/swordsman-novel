# Chapter 14

The Council of Necessity met two days later in a smaller room on the same floor.

Nine people around a table built for twelve. The brothers against the wall again. Maren seated this time. She had asserted standing and no one had successfully objected.

Casver was present. He had standing as a branch member. He had not spoken yet.

---

"The matter before this council," Lord Aldric said, "is whether the road incident constitutes military necessity. I will hear positions."

Drevant spoke first.

"The maintenance zone question has not been resolved since the full court session. Until it is, any recommendation from this council rests on an unclear jurisdictional foundation. I am not blocking a recommendation. I am noting a procedural gap that the Review Tribunal will raise if we don't address it here."

"The maintenance zone agreement is administrative," Venne said. "It has no bearing on military authorization. The council's mandate is the necessity question. I'd like us to address it."

Jianxin looked at Drevant. His body was angled toward the door the same as it had been two days ago.

"Any military response requires a formal Assessment of Interest," Orath said. "That assessment hasn't been completed."

Drevant leaned forward slightly. "On that point, there is also the matter of—"

"The road is still blocked," Cael said.

Drevant sat back.

Aldren said: "The Council's mandate is to assess the question properly. That's what we're doing."

Cael looked at his brother and said nothing.

---

The council called Venne as a vassal impact witness. She stood without being invited to and read from the papers she'd brought.

"Three merchant houses. Four supply routes. Seventeen days of disruption." She read a figure in coin, plainly, without apology. "I can provide the breakdown by route if the council requires it. I have it here."

No one asked for the breakdown. The figure sat in the room.

The path to authorization was briefly clear.

---

"Before the council finalizes its recommendation," Casver said, "I want to raise a question about the evidentiary basis for the proceeding. As Lord Drevant has noted, the maintenance zone interpretation affects which testimony can be treated as disinterested. The account of the attack rests substantially on the testimony of two witnesses, the individuals present with Lady Maren, retained persons with a contractual relationship with the house. Their account may be entirely accurate. I raise this not to dispute it, but because the Review Tribunal will ask whether conclusions drawn from interested-party testimony were appropriately qualified. Better to address it here than have the tribunal return the recommendation on procedural grounds."

The room considered this.

Jianxin looked at Drevant. Drevant's expression had not changed. He had not noted anything about the maintenance zone interpretation and testimony. He had been cut off before he could.

"The witnesses are available," Maren said.

"Of course. And their availability for formal attestation should be noted in the record. What I'm raising is a matter of evidentiary classification, not credibility. The council should be transparent about what its conclusions rest on."

Jianxin looked at Maren.

She was looking at the table. Her hands were flat on the surface in front of her, both of them, not one. Something had moved in her face when Casver raised the evidentiary point, small, controlled, already going before it had fully arrived. What replaced it was a composure so total it sat differently than her usual composure, closed where her usual stillness was open.

---

The Council recommended a Review Tribunal.

The tribunal would assess the council's findings and the evidentiary basis before any military authorization could proceed. Five days to constitute. Ten days to present findings to the full court. The road would remain as it was until then.

Lord Aldric's jaw set once and released. He thanked the council for its diligence. He called the session closed.

Venne Serrath gathered her papers slowly. She did not look at anyone as she did it.

---

A servant found them in the corridor twenty minutes later. Lady Maren's study. When they were ready.

The study was three doors down from the council room. Maren was standing at the window when they entered, back to them. She turned when they came in and Jianxin caught it in the first second before the composure reasserted. The set of her shoulders. The way she'd been holding herself while she waited.

On the road, at swordpoint, she had been steadier than this. Two strangers in a treeline, thirty men down, a carriage at a wrong angle. She had stepped out and assessed it all without a crack. That composure hadn't shifted since. Not once.

It had shifted now.

She sat. They sat.

"This is why I hired you," she said. "Not the escort work. I need support from the vassal tier. Enough to call a formal authority review without it collapsing before it starts. Drevant, Serrath, Orath. Each of them has something that needs to move. I need you to make that happen."

Tianyi had stopped looking at the bookshelves. His eyes were moving somewhere past the spines, quick and unfocused, a slight blur to them, like a man reading several things at once that only he could see.

"Are you going to tell us what the authority review is for?" Jianxin said.

She looked at him. "Not particularly."

"Emergency authority under Clause Fourteen," Tianyi said.

The room was very quiet.

"Your father hasn't used his co-signature in four years. Not on anything that counts." He turned a page on the shelf without removing it. "There's a provision. Dormancy. If it's lapsed, the authority passes to you by default. You don't need the vote. You may already have it." A pause. "The vassal work isn't building toward a vote. It's clearing the ground so that when you move, there's nothing in the room that can stop it cheaply." He looked at her for the first time. "It's a far-fetched plan."

Maren was looking at him. For a moment, just a moment, she looked genuinely shocked. Her uncle had been blocking her at every turn for months and hadn't found it.

"You read the charter," she said.

"The copy in the library. Two days ago."

"The full document."

"It took an afternoon."

Something recalibrated in her face. Small. Controlled. She had been the person who knew the documents in every room for two years. She adjusted without comment.

"Everything that cannot work has been removed," she said. "What remains is what I'm doing."

Jianxin looked at her. He had heard that before, or the shape of it. Not the words but the structure underneath them, the frictionless certainty of someone who had genuinely eliminated alternatives rather than suppressed them. Not arguing. Not hoping. Describing what was left.

Garson had written like that. The reasoning moves because it has nowhere else to go.

"What are you trying to protect?" Jianxin said.

"The family's position. The marriage—"

"I know what the marriage does."

The room was quiet.

"My sister is twelve," she said. "She hasn't decided yet what kind of person to be." She stopped there.

Tianyi had turned from the bookshelves. "The dinner. What do you need beyond Drevant."

"That's enough for now," Maren said.

Jianxin stood. He looked at her, at the hands flat on the desk, at the dark circles under the precise hair, at the face that had been almost completely sealed when she stepped out of a carriage on a forest road and was almost completely sealed again.

Almost.

"We'll find out what changed," he said.

She nodded once. The session was closed.

---

They walked back through the corridor without speaking. The household staff moved around them with the quiet of people who knew when not to ask questions.

Jianxin was looking at the floor.

Tianyi looked at him, then at the closed council room door behind them, then forward again. Something about the session was still sitting in him wrong. Venne's numbers in the room and then not in the room. The road still blocked.

"It's not a bad system," Tianyi said. "Lord Aldric can't just send soldiers because he wants to. Everyone with standing gets addressed. That's — that prevents the strongest party from doing whatever they want regardless of everyone else." He was looking at the corridor ahead. "The outcome is bad. The logic isn't."

"I know," Jianxin said.

"You look like you disagree."

"Partly." He kept walking. "But that's not what I'm thinking about."

Tianyi looked at him. "Then what."

"Casver referenced something Drevant noted," Jianxin said. "About the maintenance zone interpretation and testimony."

"Yes. Drevant raised the maintenance zone question in the full court. It's on the record."

"Drevant didn't finish the point in there." Jianxin gestured back toward the council room. "He started to and Cael cut him off. He never said anything about testimony."

Tianyi was quiet.

"Casver knew what he was going to say," Jianxin said. "Before he said it."

"It's possible to anticipate an argument without prior coordination. Drevant's position has been consistent. The connection between the maintenance zone question and evidentiary weight is logical."

"Yes," Jianxin said.

They walked further.

"Maren knew the challenge was coming," Jianxin said. "Before he made it. Her hands."

Tianyi looked at him. "You're certain."

"Both flat, not one. She was holding something down. She knew."

Tianyi was quiet for a moment. "If she knew and couldn't prevent it, she's been reading him for longer than we've been here."

They walked the rest of the way without speaking. At the end of the corridor Jianxin looked back once at the closed door of the council room. The road was still blocked. The court had been correct at every step. Nobody in either room had done anything wrong.

He thought about Venne gathering her papers without looking at anyone. He thought about Casver's expression, helpful and engaged, the surface running continuously because that was what it did.

He didn't say any of this to Tianyi.

Tianyi would identify the design problem precisely. What Jianxin was looking at wasn't a design problem.
