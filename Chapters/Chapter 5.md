# Chapter 5

The arena at the institute's center was empty at this hour, the stained glass throwing its fractured colors across the stone floor without anyone to receive them. Jianxin had come early on purpose. He liked the space before other people filled it, when it was still possible to hear the building breathe.

Tianyi arrived exactly when he said he would, which was always.

They didn't speak much before a spar. There was a ritual to it, a quality of returning to something established so long ago that the establishing itself was gone. Jianxin rolled his shoulders. Tianyi settled into his stance, and the air around him shifted in the way it always did when he drew on the Dao, a subtle change in pressure that made the room feel slightly larger and slightly less forgiving.

Jianxin raised his sword and felt the familiar weight of it, and grinned.

Tianyi opened with fire, a feint toward Jianxin's left that was never meant to land, its purpose to pull the eye while a current of wind came low and fast along the ground. Jianxin read the fire for what it was, stepped back from the wind, and drove forward into the gap it left. His blade came through fast and low, the angle of an old swordsman's cut he'd absorbed so thoroughly it no longer felt borrowed, and Tianyi had to redirect a full step sideways to avoid it.

First blood to neither of them, but Tianyi had moved. That was something.

They reset and came at each other again. Tianyi's web began to build the way it always did, patient and distributed, fire drawing the attention here while water pressured the footing there, earth anchoring his center while wind moved through the gaps in Jianxin's guard looking for purchase. None of it was decisive on its own. All of it together made the arena feel like it was slowly tilting.

Jianxin moved through it the way he'd always moved through it, sword shifting between registers mid-exchange, the duelist's footwork carrying him inside a water strike before becoming the stubborn mountain's planted stance when Tianyi's wind tried to redirect his momentum. He wasn't one swordsman. He never had been. The arts flowed through him without discord, each one arriving when the moment asked for it, and the combination was difficult to model because there was no consistent baseline to model from.

He drove Tianyi back twice in the first exchange, clean aggressive pressure that forced a real response rather than a redirect. The second time Tianyi's earth qi surged to anchor him and he stopped moving but his guard was high, and Jianxin swept low and felt the tip of his blade graze Tianyi's outer robe before wind caught his wrist and turned the strike aside.

Close. Closer than it had been in the garden two days ago.

Tianyi's expression didn't change, but the web tightened. More attention on the low line now. Jianxin noted it and filed it away, and began doing the other thing he'd come here to practice.

He was watching for the opening in Tianyi's web, not to attack through it but to show it to his brother. To plant something. The difference between how he'd fought in the garden and how he was fighting now wasn't ability, not entirely. It was that he'd stopped looking for a way through the web and started looking for a way to make Tianyi look at a particular door long enough to stop watching all the others.

He came in hard on the left, a feint he committed to just enough to be believable, and when Tianyi's fire moved to cut off the angle Jianxin shifted his weight and let his blade present a gap, a fraction of an opening on the right side that would have rewarded a faster commitment.

Tianyi didn't take it. He was too precise for that.

But something in the web's shape shifted slightly around that angle. He had seen it. He'd registered it as a possibility, and the registration had cost him a fraction of the attention he'd been using to manage the water pressure on the left.

Jianxin stepped into the water.

It caught his back foot and his stance stuttered. Tianyi's wind came through the gap and struck across his forearm, controlled but real, and Jianxin felt it the way you feel evidence. Sharp and particular and now part of the record.

He rolled his arm and kept going.

What followed was the best exchange they'd had. Jianxin pushing, Tianyi holding and redirecting, the rhythm faster than the garden spar had been, both of them a little more serious than the occasion probably called for. Jianxin's blade found Tianyi's guard three times in quick succession, two of them turned aside cleanly and the third glancing off his shoulder before earth qi absorbed the force. Tianyi's lightning came once, singular and decisive, aimed at the moment Jianxin's footwork crossed, and Jianxin moved just enough that it caught his side instead of his center, the force radiating through his ribs and stealing a breath.

He stepped back and took that breath, and Tianyi let him, which was generous and slightly condescending and very Tianyi.

"You're faster," Tianyi said. It was an observation, not a compliment.

"I know." Jianxin settled back into his stance. "You're compensating on the left. Your water comes earlier than it used to when I pressure that side."

A pause. "You've been counting."

"I've been reading." He raised his sword. "Come on."

The next sequence Jianxin kept the threat alive, the unresolved opening on the right that Tianyi now had to keep accounting for. He could feel the web adjusting around it the same way the Go pattern had bent around the stone in the upper left, the distributed pressure that had made Tianyi's style so suffocating beginning to organize around a fixed point.

Fixed points were legible. He'd learned that last night across a board game, and it was good to know it held here too.

Tianyi felt it the way he felt most things, as a variable that needed accounting for.

The opening on Jianxin's right wasn't an accident. He'd understood that within two exchanges of its first appearance. A genuine gap in Jianxin's form would have closed by now, corrected by instinct if nothing else. This one stayed open, consistent in its dimensions, patient in the way that only deliberate things were patient. Which meant Jianxin knew it was there, which meant committing to it was committing to whatever Jianxin had prepared on the other side.

So he didn't commit to it. He accounted for it, which was different, and the accounting had a cost he was already paying in attention he'd rather have spent elsewhere.

He adjusted the web and kept building.

He drove forward again, harder than before, and this time Tianyi's response had a different quality. The web was still there but pulling slightly inward, converging toward the right where the open angle lived, pressure on the left thinning to compensate. Jianxin felt the shift the way you feel a change in current underwater, not dramatic, just the resistance moving.

He swept left through the thinned pressure and Tianyi had to bring fire across to cover it, a bigger commitment than he'd have made ten minutes ago. The flame came in wide and Jianxin ducked under it and drove his elbow into the space where Tianyi's guard had been a half second earlier, not a sword strike but an interruption, and Tianyi stepped back and reset and for just a moment his expression carried something that wasn't quite calculation.

They circled. Jianxin's forearm still ached from the wind hit and his ribs from the lightning, and he breathed through both and kept his face easy.

"Your fire's getting wider," he said.

Tianyi said nothing, which was confirmation enough.

He came in again, this time threatening the open angle on the right and simultaneously pressing low on the left, forcing Tianyi to split between two lines at once. Water came to cover the low threat and fire moved toward the right, and for a moment Tianyi was managing both and the web had pulled tight around two points instead of spreading freely. The distributed pressure that made his style so hard to fight directly had become a balancing act, and balancing acts had weight on both sides.

Jianxin pushed the left pressure harder and watched the right coverage thin.

He pushed the right and watched the left thin.

He did this three more times, probing the balance, learning the rhythm of how Tianyi's attention moved between the two threats, until he understood it the way he understood the weight behind the calligrapher's strokes. Not consciously, not as calculation. Just as a shape he could feel the edges of.

Then he set up the shoulder.

He let Tianyi land the hit he'd been setting up, moved into it rather than away from it, and felt the sharpness of it spread down his arm. His guard on the right dropped exactly as far as it was going to drop. He'd been building toward this moment across the entire spar, the record now complete, the evidence sufficient, and there was only one thing left that the evidence required.

He stopped thinking about it.

The clarity arrived differently than it had in his room with Garson's manual. There it had come through accumulation and hours. Here it came through completeness, the same logic but faster, compressed into the weight of everything that had landed on him across the spar. The forearm. The ribs. The shoulder just now. What remained when everything that could not be was removed was a single line from where he stood to where Tianyi's guard had opened.

His blade moved.

It had none of the music of his usual sword. The grip was too deliberate, the angle too considered, the weight behind it not instinct but conclusion. It arrived at Tianyi's guard with the finality of a door being closed, unhurried, carrying exactly the force the verdict required.

Tianyi's guard gave.

Jianxin's blade came to rest at his brother's throat.

The arena was quiet. The colored light from the stained glass lay patient and indifferent across the floor between them.

Then the state broke, and Jianxin became aware of his arm, the sharp ache spreading from the shoulder hit radiating down through his side. His ribs joined the conversation a moment later. More than he'd registered during the strike itself, significantly more, and the sound that came out of him was not something he'd planned.

Tianyi's eyes moved from the blade at his throat to his brother's face.

"How bad," he said, and didn't wait for an answer before his hand was already at Jianxin's side, checking.

Jianxin hissed. "That's where it hurts, thanks."

"I know where it hurts, I'm the one who hit you there." Tianyi's hands moved to his shoulder next, then his arm, quick and matter-of-fact, the way you check something you already know is wrong and just need to confirm. "You took that hit on purpose."

"Brilliant tactical decision-making."

"You're an idiot." He pulled Jianxin's arm gently away from his body to get a better look, and Jianxin let him. "Can you move it properly?"

Jianxin demonstrated, which produced another sound he hadn't planned.

"Right." Tianyi pulled his outer robe off and dropped it over his brother's shoulders, then got under his good arm without discussion. "Infirmary."

"It's not that serious."

"You just made that noise twice."

"That was a tactical noise."

"Jianxin."

"Fine." He let Tianyi take some of his weight, which was easier than he'd expected and more comfortable than he'd admit. "You're going to bring this up forever, aren't you."

"Probably," Tianyi said, and walked him out through the fractured colored light, the stained glass warriors watching them go with their usual patience and indifference.
