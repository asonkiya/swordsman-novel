# Chapter 6

The infirmary was a single room at the back of the institute, small and practical, with two cots and a cabinet of medicinal supplies that the clan restocked every few months without anyone coming to restock it. Jianxin had noticed this about the institute in general. Things appeared when they were needed and disappeared when they weren't, and asking where they came from had never produced a satisfying answer. The instructors deflected. The supplies just came. At some point he and Tianyi had stopped asking.

Tianyi sat him down on the nearer cot and began going through the cabinet with the same systematic efficiency he brought to everything, setting aside what he wanted and leaving the rest in order.

"You don't have to do that," Jianxin said.

"I know."

"I can manage."

"You demonstrated your ability to manage approximately two minutes ago when you made that noise going down the steps."

"The steps were uneven."

"They're the same steps we've used our whole lives."

Jianxin considered arguing further and decided against it. His ribs had opinions about deep breaths and his shoulder had opinions about everything else, and Tianyi was already heating water with a small application of fire qi so controlled it was almost domestic. He watched his brother work and said nothing for a while.

The gruel came together with the same precision as everything Tianyi did. Medicinal herbs measured and added in a specific order, the heat kept low and steady, the consistency checked twice. Jianxin had seen this process before, on the handful of occasions over the years when one of them had come out of a spar worse than expected. Tianyi never made a production of it. He just cooked, and the result was something that tasted like concentrated effort and vaguely of ginger, and worked better than it had any right to given how unpleasant it was to eat.

"You could just let me cook it," Jianxin said.

"The last time you cooked the medicinal gruel you substituted two of the ingredients because you thought they smelled wrong."

"They did smell wrong."

"They're supposed to smell wrong. That's how you know they're working." Tianyi set the bowl on the small table beside the cot. "Eat it."

Jianxin looked at it. "It smells wrong."

"Yes."

He ate it. It tasted like it smelled, which was consistent if not pleasant, and after a few minutes he could feel the qi beginning to move through the bruised tissue with a warmth that meant it was doing what it was supposed to do. He set the bowl down and stretched his arm carefully, testing the range.

Better. Still sore, but better.

Tianyi had settled onto the other cot, his back against the wall, watching with the unhurried attention of someone who has decided the next thing on his list is waiting.

"The shoulder hit," Jianxin said.

"What about it."

"I set it up over the whole spar. I needed you committed enough that the guard would drop exactly that far, so I baited the angle on the right from the third exchange forward, kept it open long enough that you had to account for it, and once your attention was split between that and the low pressure I walked into the water to give you the forearm, which locked in the pattern. By the time the shoulder came you'd already decided what I was going to do next."

Tianyi was quiet for a moment. "I know. I understood most of it during the spar. Not all of it, but most."

"What did you miss."

"The forearm. I thought that was genuine." He considered. "And something changed in your sword at the end. The movement was wrong for your style. I kept trying to find it in your footwork and it wasn't there."

Jianxin turned the empty bowl in his hands. That was useful to know, that it read as stylistically foreign rather than as his own method pushed somewhere new. "The problem is I can't do it reliably yet. The way I enter it needs time to build. Against someone faster or less controlled than you that cost is going to be too high."

"So you need to find a way in with less setup."

"Or make the setup cheaper." He set the bowl down. "What about you. What did the spar tell you."

Tianyi was quiet for longer this time, which meant he was being honest rather than quick. "My approach assumes a reactive opponent. Someone who responds to positions rather than creates them. Against that kind of opponent the distributed pressure works because there's no single point they can organize around. You don't answer. You place. And I don't have a clean response to that."

"You have to commit earlier."

"I have to commit at all, which I generally avoid." He said it without self-criticism, as a factual observation about his own method. "The web works because it never fully commits. The moment it has to, it becomes legible."

Jianxin thought about this. "Same problem from the other side. You need to commit without becoming readable. I need to read without needing the setup."

Tianyi looked at him. "That's going to take years."

"We have years." Jianxin stretched his arm again, testing. "Seven of them, give or take."

"Seven at most." Tianyi looked at the wall. "Less if we don't use them well."

Jianxin was quiet for a moment. Then he grinned. "First time I've beaten you, by the way."

"You've beaten me before."

"At Go. And that's different." He settled back against the wall. "This is the first time I've beaten you at something you're actually supposed to be better at."

Tianyi said nothing, which was louder than most things Tianyi said.

"I'm going to remember this for a very long time," Jianxin said.

"I know."

"I might bring it up at inopportune moments."

"I know."

"Just so we're clear."

"We're clear." Tianyi's expression hadn't changed, but something at the edge of it had, the faint quality of someone enduring something they've already accepted as inevitable. "Are you done."

"Probably not." Jianxin looked at the ceiling. "When we start cultivating properly, at sixteen, what do you think it actually feels like?"

"I don't know. The instructors have described it in theory."

"Theory." Jianxin turned the word over. "Qi Gathering first. Holding the world inside yourself, they said. Running small pieces of it in your head until you understand how they behave."

"And then Foundation Establishment. Building the picture from the pieces."

"And then Golden Core, which is apparently where everything falls apart."

"Where the parts that were never true fall apart," Tianyi said. "What remains is stronger."

"You would find that appealing."

"It's the most honest part of the path."

Jianxin considered this. "Mine doesn't work like that. I just keep swinging and hope something interesting happens."

Tianyi looked at him. "That's your cultivation philosophy."

"It's working so far."

"You beat me once."

"Exactly." He grinned. "Something interesting happened."

Jianxin had already looked back at the ceiling, thinking about something else. Tianyi watched him for a moment, and then looked away.

Outside the single window the evening had gone fully dark, the sky the deep starless blue of a clear night, the way the sky always was on this world.

They both knew what the years meant. What they were waiting for, what had to happen before the outside world became something they could enter. The Dao out there was dense and established and old, and Tianyi's physique needed to be its own thing first, rooted enough that the outside couldn't simply overwrite it. That was what the instructors had told them. That was why they were here instead of out there, why the clan had built this whole place around two children and then left them in it.

When Tianyi reached that point, they could leave. Then they could go home.

Tianyi looked at him. "You have time."

"We have time," Jianxin said.

Tianyi looked at him for a moment, and then briefly something in his expression did what it rarely did, softened past the precision into something quieter and less guarded. It didn't last long. But Jianxin saw it, and stored it away the way he stored everything, in a place he'd be able to find it later.

"Get some sleep," Tianyi said. "Your shoulder needs it."

Jianxin lay back on the cot, careful of his shoulder, and looked at the ceiling. The infirmary was quiet around them.

Seven years.

He closed his eyes, and thought about everything he didn't know yet, and how much he was going to enjoy finding out.
