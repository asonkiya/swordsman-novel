# Chapter 13

They sat in the courtyard until the household finished waking around them, the groom resuming his rounds, the kitchen girls picking up their conversation from wherever they'd dropped it. The brazier stopped bending. The grass stood up again.

Tianyi sat on the low wall near the training post. He had his eyes closed and his palm extended in front of him.

Five things moved around his hand.

A tongue of flame, a coil of water drawn from the morning damp in the air, a thin ribbon of wind that made the flame lean without extinguishing it, a sliver of stone peeled from the wall's surface, and a thread of something darker and denser that Jianxin didn't have a name for. They circled his hand like a coin swimming across fingers, no effort in it, each element finding the next without being pushed, passing through each other's space without interference, the sequence running on its own.

"The elemental workings are different," Tianyi said, eyes still closed. "Before, I was pushing. Now it's more like asking." The five elements shifted their formation without breaking rhythm, the flame moving to the outside, the water folding to the center. "In the forest that would have taken concentration. Just now I was mostly talking."

He opened his eyes and let them disperse. The water fell as a fine mist. The flame went out. The stone chip dropped back to the wall.

He looked at the training post. A root beneath the courtyard stones shifted. Jianxin felt the faint vibration through his feet before he saw the flagstone nearest the post lift a finger's width, tilt, and settle back. "In the forest I could feel the earth in sections. Roughly. Push here, resist there." He pressed his palm flat against the wall. "Now I can feel the composition. The stone here has a seam running northeast, about two handspans down. There's water pooling in a hollow beneath the far corner of the yard." He paused. "The training post has been set in ground that drains poorly. It will rot at the base within a few years."

"Does it do anything useful to know that?"

"Knowing where the weaknesses are in a foundation is always useful." He stood. "The bottleneck." He picked up a pebble from the ground and set it on the wall, then produced a small flame above his open palm, no larger than a candle's. He tilted it, watched it correct, tilted it again. "There's a transition from third level to fourth that's considered significant. The texts describe it as a period of stagnation, comprehension and Qi accumulation falling out of alignment, the cultivator having to force them back into correspondence. It can take months."

The flame held its shape without wavering. He tilted his palm slightly. The flame tilted with it, staying anchored, then drifted sideways at his direction through empty air and circled the pebble once before he closed his hand and it went out.

"I didn't notice it. I went from third to fourth in two days and felt nothing the texts described."

"Maybe you missed it."

"I didn't miss it. The transition requires a specific kind of resistance, the framework pushing against an upper boundary and having to reconfigure around it. I would have felt that." He looked at Jianxin. "I think my comprehension was already so far past the boundary that by the time my Qi accumulation reached it, there was nothing left to resist. The framework had already been built on the other side."

Jianxin thought about this. "So the bottlenecks don't slow you down because you've already done the work before you arrive."

"Something like that." A pause. "There are two more transitions like this before Foundation Establishment. Sixth to seventh, and ninth to Foundation. If the pattern holds I won't feel those either."

He looked at the wall. A thin layer of frost formed along its surface, slow and even, then melted. He was checking something again, satisfied by whatever answer the checking gave him, and moved on without commenting on it.

The kitchen door opened and the smell of breakfast came through. Jianxin looked at his brother and found him precisely himself, sitting on a low wall before sunrise with frost melting off the stone behind him and the world still slightly denser around him than around anyone else.

"You're going to be very difficult to fight in about six months," Jianxin said.

Tianyi looked at him. "Six months." He stood up and brushed the frost off the wall behind him. "Your sword is right there."

---

They didn't spar. They went inside, argued about whether the frost demonstration counted as showing off, and ate breakfast. Maren was already at the table, correspondence spread in front of her, a cup of tea going cold at her elbow.

She was dressed precisely, dark jacket, high-collared, hair pinned back without a strand out of place. The dressing required a decision and she had executed it correctly. The dark circles under her eyes said the decision had cost something she hadn't fully had to spare. She looked up when they came in, noted them, returned to her correspondence without breaking the motion.

Jianxin sat down and reached for the bread. Tianyi sat down and looked at the correspondence arrangement, already building whatever picture it gave him.

"The family court convenes this morning," she said. "You'll attend as part of my household. You won't speak unless spoken to." She paused. "You may be spoken to."

"What happens in a family court?" Jianxin said.

"The family and its vassals deliberate on matters of shared concern. Each party with standing may speak. Decisions require consensus." She picked up her tea, found it cold, set it back down. "It moves slowly."

She said the last part in a tone that told him everything the explanation hadn't.

---

The chamber was on the upper floor, a long room with windows on the south side and a table that seated twenty. The family at the head, Lord Aldric centered, Aldren to his right, the vassals arranged by standing down the length of the table. Maren's household staff positioned along the wall, and with them the brothers. Jianxin put his back against the stone and read the room as people settled.

The seating wasn't arbitrary. Each person's distance from Lord Aldric was a measure of their standing, the angles of the chairs calibrated to make some conversations easier and others harder. He'd seen the city arranged the same way from the carriage window.

Lord Drevant came in first, a compact man who found his seat without looking for it. His eyes went to Lord Aldric, then to Casver, in that order.

Venne Serrath arrived shortly after with papers under her arm, already arranging them before she'd fully sat down.

Two more vassal representatives took seats on the far side of the table. Orath, a thin man who traded in relationships rather than territory, and a younger representative whose name Jianxin didn't catch. Neither of them looked at Casver when they came in. Drevant had.

Then Casver Vayne came through the door.

Not last, not first. Jianxin watched him find his seat and felt himself reaching before he'd decided to.

What he found was a surface. Not unreadable, he could feel something underneath, moving, present. But the surface itself was constructed, maintained, running continuously. The man had been walking to his seat in a room full of family. There was no one watching him closely. Nothing was at stake yet. And still every gesture was placed, every expression managed. He adjusted his cuffs before sitting. He set his papers down squarely. He looked at the room with the same measured attention he would have given a room that required it.

Jianxin had met people who performed in public and relaxed in private. He had met people who performed always because they were always afraid. This was neither of those. This was a habit so old it had stopped requiring effort, a person who concealed without deciding to, because it was simply what they did.

He watched Casver take his seat and kept watching.

---

Lord Aldric reached beneath the table and set a small brass scale on the surface before him. The brass was dark at the edges, the pan on the left stained from use. The right pan caught the light differently, polished, wiped clean after each use in a way the left pan wasn't. He drew a small ritual knife from his belt and opened his palm. Three drops of blood fell onto the left pan. The scale tilted and held.

He did not close the wound.

"Under the eye of the Witness, all things have purpose."

The room: "Under the eye of the Witness, all things have purpose."

A drop fell.

"Under the hand of the Plague Bearer, all things have cost."

"Under the hand of the Plague Bearer, all things have cost."

A drop fell.

"Under the law of the Arbiter of Necessity, all things have their place."

"Under the law of the Arbiter of Necessity, all things have their place."

"Necessity does not ask."

A beat. The blood still falling.

"To it we offer —"

"Ourselves, without remainder."

He reached beneath the table and placed a short carved rod on the right pan. The scale fell completely to the right. The blood side rose.

"Then let us begin."

He wrapped his hand. Lord Aldric sat. The room sat.

---

"The matter before this court," Lord Aldric said, "is an organized assault on a member of this house on a road under our protection. Not brigandry. A coordinated action requiring a coordinated answer."

Before anyone could respond, Casver spoke.

"With respect to the proceedings — I note the presence of two individuals whose standing before this court has not been established. Given the circumstances that bring us here, it seems appropriate to clarify their involvement before we proceed. Their origins, their affiliations, whether any party with interest in the outcome might have reason to place capable individuals in proximity to our household." He spread his hands slightly. "I raise this not to impugn anyone. Only because the court's deliberations should rest on a foundation of certainty."

The room waited.

"Their origins," Casver said, looking at Jianxin. "Where did you come from?"

Jianxin answered. Then: "Would it be useful to know what we observed about the attack? The number of men, their positions, the coordination?"

"That will come in due course," Casver said. "Your affiliations — do you hold obligations to any house or faction with business in Aldenmere?"

"No," Jianxin said. "Do you want to know about the man directing them from horseback? He didn't engage directly."

A pause. "That is noted. And patrons — is there anyone who might have arranged your presence on that road?"

Tianyi answered the backing question in two sentences. Flat and complete.

Casver nodded and moved on.

"They are under my protection," Maren said. "Their presence is at my discretion."

Casver inclined his head and picked up his cup. His eyes were already on the correspondence in front of him.

---

The road took another hour to properly arrive as a subject.

"The attack occurred in a shared maintenance zone," Drevant said. "My house holds the eastern portion under an agreement that has never been formally renewed. Any military action affecting the zone requires consultation with all parties to the original agreement."

"The maintenance zone agreement is administrative," Venne said. "Conflating it with military authority would require renegotiating every maintenance arrangement in the district before any house could act on its own road."

"Any military response requires a formal Assessment of Interest from all affected parties before authorization can proceed," Orath said.

"The Assessment itself requires establishing which parties have sufficient stake to constitute an affected party," the younger representative said.

"The road is blocked," Cael said. "Men attacked my sister's carriage. That's the necessity."

"There is a precedent," Aldren said. "In the third year of the Family Head's tenure, House Vayne mobilized soldiers without full court authorization. The political cost took four years to resolve. The Council of Necessity exists specifically to prevent a recurrence."

Cael looked at his brother. He didn't say anything.

Casver waited until the room had settled.

"A point of clarification," he said. "Has Lord Aldric's reaffirmation of active force authorization been filed for the current administrative cycle? I raise it only because the Charter requires it on record before any authorization can proceed. I have no reason to think it hasn't been filed. I simply want to ensure the record is complete."

"It has been filed," Lord Aldric said.

"Of course." Casver wrote something down.

Jianxin watched him write it.

The court produced one actionable outcome: a Council of Necessity, to be convened within two days, charged with determining whether the road incident met the threshold for military response.

Lord Aldric called the session closed.

---

The room emptied. Jianxin walked beside Tianyi toward the door and said nothing for long enough that Tianyi looked at him.

"What," Tianyi said.

"The questions."

"Were reasonable. Two unknown individuals in a family court after an attack on a family member. The affiliations question, the patrons question — legitimate security concerns."

"If you genuinely suspected we were a threat to Maren," Jianxin said, "what's the first thing you'd ask?"

Tianyi thought about it. "What happened on the road. What the threat looked like. Whether you had any read on who sent them."

"He didn't ask any of that."

Tianyi was quiet.

"He had no more information than anyone else in the room. But he skipped past the obvious questions entirely. Went straight to affiliations, patrons, connections to houses with business in Aldenmere." Jianxin watched Casver exchange a few words with Drevant near the window. "Those aren't the questions you ask when you're worried about Maren's safety. Those are the questions you ask when you already know the answers to the obvious ones."

Tianyi looked at him. He didn't say anything for a moment. "That's an inference."

"Yes."

"The questions were still reasonable on their face."

"They were." Jianxin watched Casver finish his conversation and move toward the door. "Tell me about the jurisdiction question."

Tianyi told him about the jurisdiction question. It took until they reached the stairs.
